A separate agent got the recipe as a one-page brief and a read-only rule for the other repository. In 50 minutes it authored a 4,190-frame trace from scratch: boot, title, New Game, the first town scene, into the house, then real play. It crossed the room and climbed the stairs, talked to the mother (every door in that room is locked by the story until you do), left through the bedroom door and took control in the next room.

Four replays across two batches ended on the same frame, tick, RNG state and state hash.

What carried over unchanged

  • Check determinism first.
  • Name state before playing: known globals, then mark, act, diff on state dumps.
  • Conditions written before any search; fresh replays as the judge; a look at the frames.

What had to change

  • Search became parameter sweeps: a scripted plan with one number varied, five to eight replays per batch. The door took one sweep (holding Right 68 frames misses it; 72 to 84 work).
  • 13 batches, 73 replays, 279,740 frames. Three quarters or more of every replay was the 2,810-frame prefix before control.
  • Reading the game mattered more: the exit only opened after the agent read the mother's dialogue off a cropped screenshot.

What that target needs most, by the agent's own measure: a snapshot at "control gained", which would cut each probe from about 3,500 frames to a few hundred and make exploration search possible; then a per-frame condition with stop-when-true.
